**The pie chart shows different attitudes** young people have **toward** increasingly intense competition in the workplace. Over two fifths of the respondents say they will strive hard to boost their competitiveness by learning continuously, **while** 38.5% are following the "lying flat" trend, without any desire to earn more than what is needed to feed themselves. **The remaining** 19.8% will resign their positions once they feel exploited by their employers.

Personalities and living conditions are **the major contributing factors in** young people's decision-making. For those who pursue excellence, constant learning and competition that allows them to enjoy the sense of self-improvement or outperformance over their colleagues can be more profitable. **In contrast**, for those who are not very ambitious, "lying flat" also has its appeal as they see little hope for upward mobility, they would rather live an idle life than join in what they see as an endless and meaningless rat race. There are **also** those who simply cannot afford to "lie flat". For them, hard work is a necessary and probably the most promising path to higher standards of living.

**When it comes to coping with a hyper-competitive work environment**, we must weigh up the pros and cons of all possible options. However, there is no doubt that pushing ourselves to the point of overwork causes harm, but so does indulging in a state of inactivity. In view of this, a smarter way would be to "lie down" for a while when we feel burned out before "standing up" again.
